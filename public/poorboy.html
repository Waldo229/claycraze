The Poor Boy

Adaptation and Persistence.

A kiln for the resourceful

Some of the strongest lessons in ceramics emerge not from abundance, but from limitation. Necessity, they say, is the mother of invention, and so it goes with the Poor Boy.

Making do with what is available is an honest way to learn about the limits and possibilities of fuel-fired kilns.

The Poor Boy represents experimentation, constraint, learning, adaptation, and persistence—the practical side of developing a studio practice.

The Poor Boy is about atmosphere.
