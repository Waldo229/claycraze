Where the work takes form

Making starts with a place to work, and opinions on what makes a good workspace differ from person to person. I’ve worked in schools, community centers, private studios, and cooperative workshops. Each has its own advantages and limitations. I prefer the freedom of my modest home studio. Working at home gives me the most flexibility. I can be creative on my own terms without constraints. The price of freedom, however, is that I’m responsible for everything that happens in the studio, including not only producing the art but also doing the work of the studio assistant. I mix the clay and glazes, fire the kilns, and manage clean-up. GENE, the kiln watcher and studio spirit, arose organically to become a sounding board for ideas and a conduit for observation. So, in this sense, my studio is unique, my own world.
