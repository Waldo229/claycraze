The Artist

Jim Alexander

Since 1973, I have crafted pottery with a deep appreciation for Japanese-influenced ceramics. My work reflects a lifelong passion for form and function, inspired by traditional Japanese aesthetics and the balance of nature.

After retiring from the Portsmouth Fire Department, I spent three years at the Norfolk Botanical Garden and maintained a small studio at the Hermitage Museum, where I rekindled my dedication to pottery.

Today, I create ceramic containers for my garden and offer them to members of my local bonsai club as well as Ikebana International Chapter 1 in Washington, D.C.

A key focus of my work is using natural clay sourced from Yorktown, Virginia—the same clay that William Rogers used in Colonial times. I call this material “Colonial Clay” to honor the region’s history and heritage while crafting pottery that enhances the beauty and spirit of bonsai arrangements.
