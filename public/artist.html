The Artist

Jim Alexander

Since 1973, I have made pottery with a deep appreciation for Japanese ceramics. My work explores form and function, drawing inspiration from traditional Japanese aesthetics and the natural world.

After retiring from the Portsmouth Fire Department, I spent three years at the Norfolk Botanical Garden and maintained a small studio at the Hermitage Museum. Those experiences renewed my dedication to pottery.

I am particularly drawn to natural clay from Yorktown, Virginia. My newfound interest in this local material connects my practice to the region’s long history of pottery making—from the Indigenous peoples of the Chesapeake’s rivers to the colonial pottery of William Rogers, remembered as the “Poor Potter of Yorktown.” My own explorations and creations are a small continuation of that history.

Today, I work from my home studio, making containers for bonsai, kusamono, and ikebana compositions, along with other objects in clay. I am a member of my local bonsai club, the Norfolk Botanical Garden, and Ikebana International. I frequently contribute to Empty Bowls events, which support efforts to relieve hunger and food insecurity, and enjoy participating in club activities. These connections continue to take me to new places and introduce me to wonderful people.
